Normative reasoning alone, deciding what a rule should be from legal doctrine, is not enough to make good policy. Regulatory impact assessment, as the OECD frames it, asks what a rule will actually do, to whom and at what cost, and it draws on more than one discipline.

Participation should be meaningful, not a formality: the people a rule affects should be heard before it is made, not after.

The values I work by follow from this: rational autonomy, evidence-based and impact-driven thinking, integrity and justice. In this lab that means results are measured against a sample I check by hand. None of it works if the underlying data is stale ([[Law is a database problem.]]), or if rules, once made, are not kept ([[The law exists because promises break.]]).
